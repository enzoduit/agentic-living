## On the Night in the Mountains

There are moments when you understand something so completely that your body reacts before your mind has time to process it.

I remember one of those nights. Zell am See — one of my favourite places in the Austrian mountains — January 2025. I had been reading a research paper on my reMarkable. The ReAct framework. Reasoning and Action. A paper describing how an AI system could reason through a problem, take an action, observe what happened, and reason again — in a loop, autonomously, indefinitely.

I was completely shaking.

My heart rate was way beyond normal — I track it with my Garmin 24/7. The kind of heart rate you get before a race, or in a moment of real fear. I needed to go outside. Not for air — I needed to move, because the thoughts spinning up in my head were too fast for a sitting body.

It was a beautiful night with the moon shining bright. Freaking cold. Ten, fifteen degrees below zero. I just did not notice it, because I was internally on fire. I walked and talked to myself out loud, the way you do when you are trying to understand something by hearing yourself say it. This paper described a system that could replicate whatever a human could do. Not a specific task. Not a narrow skill. The general loop: observe, reason, act. The loop that is the foundation of almost everything we call knowledge work.

If that loop could be automated, what exactly was left?

I had no answer that night. I walked until the cold finally reached me, then went inside. I did not sleep well.

What I did not know — what I could not have named in that moment — was that I had just crossed a threshold. I had not known it was there. I had not been trying to cross it. But somewhere on that walk, in the minus fifteen degrees, in the dark, reading a research paper by moonlight, I went from not understanding what was now possible to understanding it completely. That night, without any vocabulary for it yet, I went from Level 0 to Level 1.

## Two Levels

Let me explain what I mean by that. Because this is the frame through which everything else in this book should be read.

A chatbot responds when you speak to it. An agent runs while you sleep.

There are two levels of relationship with AI and agents. Most people are at Level 0. Almost nobody talks about this distinction. But it is the most important thing I know about the moment we are living through.

**Level 0** is where you don't yet know — really know — what AI and agents can do. You may have seen demos. You may use ChatGPT. You may think you understand it reasonably well. But you have not yet felt in your body what it means for a system to build something complex, autonomously, from a single instruction — while you are doing something else entirely. You haven't watched it fail, correct itself, and produce a result that you would not have reached faster alone. Until you have experienced that, you cannot fully believe it. And because you can't fully believe it, you can't actually participate in what is being built around you. You are watching the game from outside the stadium.

**Level 1** is the moment you know. Not as an idea — as an experience. You've built something. You've watched something run while you weren't watching. You've felt — in your body, not just your mind — that the only limit is what you can imagine. At that moment, everything you learned about how to code, how to execute technically, how to do the things that execution requires — it is worth almost nothing. Not because those skills are useless in isolation. But because everyone at Level 1 has access to the same capabilities. The barrier that used to separate the people who could build from the people who couldn't — it's gone. What's left is not technical ability. What's left is the quality of your thinking, the originality of your goals, and your capacity to turn imagination into something real.

Here is the part that surprises most people when I tell them: the market at Level 1 is harder than the one you left. Not easier. When everyone can build fast, the people who are ahead are not ahead because they can build — they're ahead because they know what to build, and how to make it work commercially. At Level 1, you compete on judgment and monetization against a field where everyone is AI-enabled. It is a tougher game.

But here is what matters more than that: at Level 0, you are not playing at all.

Not because the game is unfair. Because you don't yet know it exists.

## Why I Am Writing This

I crossed from Level 0 to Level 1 alone, in the mountains, reading a paper I'd found on my own.

What followed — the year I am about to describe — is what Level 1 actually looks like in practice. Not as a concept. As a way of running a business, running an ultra in Patagonia, fixing a server crash from a beach in Spain, having coffee in the Argentine highlands while three companies kept running without me at a keyboard.

But I also started teaching the crossing.

At IMC Krems in Austria. At Queensland University of Technology in Brisbane, Australia. Three-day workshops. People from different backgrounds, different levels of technical familiarity, different relationships with technology. The goal was always the same: by the end of the third day, everyone in the room would be at Level 1.

100% success rate across every cohort.

That number is the most important sentence in this introduction. Not because it proves I am a good teacher. Because it proves the crossing is learnable. It is not a talent. It is not reserved for developers or founders or people who grew up thinking about systems. It is a threshold, and there is a path across it. I have walked people across it enough times now to know exactly where the difficult parts are, and what helps.

Peter Steinberger — the Austrian entrepreneur who built the infrastructure that made my year-long experiment possible — has a phrase I keep coming back to: *"Only imagination is holding us back."* He said it in the context of what his software could do. But I think it describes Level 1 exactly. Not just the capability. The mental shift. The moment when you stop asking *is this possible* and start asking *what should I build*.

That is the shift this book is trying to produce.

My goal — with Agentic Living, with the workshops, with everything I have built around this — is to get as many people as possible to Level 1. Not because I think everyone will thrive there. The market is harder than before, and I won't pretend otherwise. But because the alternative — staying at Level 0 in a world that is reorganising itself around Level 1 thinking — is to be excluded from a transformation that is already underway.

The Zell am See night was the moment I stopped being excluded. Everything that follows is what I found on the other side of that walk.

One note on who this is for. Some readers are building their own companies. Others are doing their best work inside existing ones. Both are at Level 0 right now. Both can cross. The destination looks different: the entrepreneur's Level 1 is a product or company that runs largely without them in the execution. The employee's Level 1 is a role that accomplishes more than any role before it — because everything that does not require their judgment is handled, and what remains is entirely their thinking. The crossing is the same. What you find on the other side is yours to define.

## How This Book Is Structured

The framework you will encounter in this book — I call it FLOW — is not a productivity system. It is a map for the crossing from Level 0 to Level 1, and for what to do once you're there.

**Awareness** is the first move: understanding that the levels exist, that you are at one of them, and what the distance between them actually means for your work and your life. You cannot cross a threshold you cannot see.

**Grounding** is building the infrastructure that makes Level 1 real for you — not theoretical, not something you've watched someone else do, but running on your behalf, in your context, producing results you own. This is not a technical exercise. It is a trust exercise.

**Elimination** is the practice that Level 1 makes possible and Level 0 makes impossible: stopping doing the things that do not require you. At Level 0, you can't stop because you don't fully believe something else can do them. At Level 1, elimination becomes obvious. The question is not *can I delegate this* — you know you can. The question is *what exactly do I do with what is returned*.

**Output** is what comes after: reinvesting the attention you have reclaimed into the things that actually require a human — judgment, creativity, presence, responsibility. The goal of Agentic Living is not efficiency. It is not even freedom, though freedom tends to follow. The goal is more of your actual self, applied to the things that actually need you.

The diary of experiments that forms the core of this book is organised around those four movements. Every story in it is an example of what they look like in practice — the successes, the failures, the moments where the system corrected itself at 3am while I slept, and the moments where nothing worked and I had to figure out why.

Read it as a field report. Not as a manifesto. Not as a how-to. As notes from someone who crossed the threshold and kept walking.

What I found is worth the walk.
