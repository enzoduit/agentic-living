## On agentic systems — full job responsibility, not just tasks

"I recently wanted to move a lot into not just single tasks or single objectives but full responsibilities for an entire job. What we currently would have is an entire employee for that case. Did I have that infrastructure where I feel like one agent is managing many other agents? The interesting part there was that these are isolated in secure instances so as not to have any data problems and so that nothing can be shared between these agents. I can manage them easily with one central agent."

## On the data security officer agent

"I was thinking around what I can do for better data security and I would try to get into the details. I was thinking, 'Yeah is actually pretty good at understanding what kind of responsibility, role, tasks, etc., a current human job has. Why not just say which human job an agent should do or add to that general agent and tell it to put itself in the role of a data security officer within that system? It can do all the jobs that the data security officer would do in order to be fully compliant and have everything documented so that we are the best and most secure company setup in the world.'

It went out, did a look at the infrastructure, etc. It actually created not just one task or automation or job but a full profile of a data security officer. In my assumption if you look at SMEs currently and how they are set up versus what this agent does, I think I'm now, with that setup, way more compliant than 99% of all companies without needing that role of a data security officer."

If you have that, as I have and I checked it, we actually have now the perfect documented compliance system, having flagged and auto-fixed stuff whenever something happens."

## On jobs and productivity

"I think the interesting part is that experts again leverage this. What previously had been done by big teams and, in the end, was faulted to two potentially human mistakes is now administered by one very smart person and executed on autopilot with agents. We have, in the end, if we look at total value creation, less input resources we need and we get a higher quality bar of the result.

You can think of this as so critical because we'll lose jobs now. Yes it's true but in the end our kind of productivity increases. If productivity increases it's then the second-order effect that we need to know how to redistribute these resources that are created and this excess productivity. In the end it's a good thing. Another bad thing is that it just creates a new challenge but a challenge that lets us as a humanity grow because we can create more total outcome due to the productivity increase. This doesn't mean that the question will be easy on a societal level but let's talk about that in the next version."

## On travelling the world — El Chaltén story

"In order to think of it as a transition and what implications this has on society, I think there's one interesting anecdote that I experienced a lot during my stay in Patagonia. Obviously there are a lot of people around that kind of do that world journey and travel the world for a year or so.

If you break it down, it was so interesting because they actually kind of quit their job, saved enough money to do that for a year or two. If you look at the world with 200+ countries, in the end if you want to see a lot and you have 365 days in one year, you can't even stay two days in a country. In the end you're just stressed.

What I'm wanting to say here is that these people actually all felt stressed but said that it's the only way to see more of the world. That was my kind of way of living. With that full independence in the physical place, I stayed for longer at all those places, enjoyed them more, and had also more opportunities, especially on things where the weather is kind of important. For example in El Chalten I stayed for two weeks and others always had just two or three days and needed to rush through the rain in order to just see Fitz Roy once.

I basically just stayed in coffee shops for a couple of days, enjoyed life, and had a super productive time. When the window opened with super nice weather, I was all out and about, seeing everything, not stressed at all, and at the same time hyper productive. Everybody actually was jealous of me and said, obviously, that's the better way of exploring the world: that I do it, staying full longer and having full flexibility, but not everybody can do that this way."

## Two effects on society

"1. More people can do it this way than we think. Most of our jobs are basically digital services. At least in the developed world it's most of the third sector. In the first sector it's most work that can be done fully digital. I think there it applies directly to this agenda living approach.

2. On the other hand we have these jobs that are just bound to the physical and probably will also stay the longest bound to the physical. Think of all the human crafts around construction, around electricity, etc., where you just don't have that flexibility so much. I think we'll have a good challenge ahead of us in order to price it correctly, if you wish, on what people earn that have physical dependencies and those that do not have them. Currently probably you even earn more, at least in many areas, in the non-physical detached world, which is at the same time super unfair to the jobs that have physical dependencies. I think we saw a first wave of that during Covid already on people that needed to be physically at certain places and those who didn't. We still don't have it priced in correctly."

"In this period of time we'll need to make sure to make these jobs attractive so that for people really wanting to do that it's also financially attractive to do so and not just kind of a 'dump' decision."

## On manual files and thinking

"One interesting exception to note here is whenever it actually makes your thinking process better and easier to use a file that you can edit. Sometimes for me I see that, trying to do a rough calculation with an Excel or Google Sheet, I need to see a couple of kinds of results of calculations fast in order to build my opinion and my thought chain fast. I think then it's totally okay to use it but I think the rule of thumb should be: whenever it enables my thinking then using a manual file type is good. Whenever I start then, I don't know, trying to make it accessible to others or format it or explain it, whatever, then it's easier to switch back to the agent. Whenever it enhances your thought process then it's totally fine. As the goal is to enhance thinking."

## Screens as means to an end

"How crazy is it that we, in the end, spend most of our time sitting in front of a screen? That is, first of all, not good for our body health because of the sitting. We try to work our way around it with a standing desk, whatever, and it is also not good for our eyes to look into screens all the time.

In the end, if we look at it, if our main task is thinking, we should question ourselves whether sitting in front of the screen is the best means to an end for the highest quality output of thinking."

"I had been thinking about that a lot: how to actually create systems of reviewing and decision-making that are fully independent from screens.

One current approach is that I spun up a decisions API that creates a list of all open decisions from my digital employees. I can call a number where I get one decision after the next with all the relevant context and ask follow-up questions and then can decide on the next step."

## On how Agentic Living feels — intensity levels (2026-07-21 dictation)

"This setup phase continues until it kind of meets my quality, where I am co-creating stuff with my agent and the agent is more kind of assistant level so that we are in kind of synchronous exchange. These sessions need to be in ultra flow, where I am super super concentrated. It just drains a lot of energy and you need to make sure that you get out of these sessions also after a certain time. If not it feels like you have five different streams open and are not achieving a single one and you're getting super super exhausted.

I think this is kind of how authentic living feels: you have these different levels of intensity where it can get super stressful if you're not mindful. If you get these blocks of flow work done, you probably get the work done in two hours that previously was a month. That's why I do these really intentionally."
