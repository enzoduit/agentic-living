## The Two Voices

This essay has two voices that alternate and contrast each other deliberately.

### Voice 1: Ed's own dictation style
Direct. Personal. Slightly unfinished sentences that feel like real thought. No over-polish.
The source material IS the style — use the dictations verbatim where possible, paraphrase only when structure requires it, and never clean up the thinking into something more "literary" than he actually said it.

Examples of RIGHT tone (from dictations):
- "I was completely shaking. I had a heart frequency that was just way beyond normal."
- "I went out for a long walk. It was a beautiful night with the moon but freaking cold, I think 10 or 15 degrees below zero. I just didn't notice it because I was just internally on fire."
- "I basically just stayed in coffee shops for a couple of days, enjoyed life, and had a super productive time."
- "My friends got used to the messages. This is absolutely crazy or I can't believe it."
- "Due to one silly mistake of not being connected with my VPN, it took longer than needed but I was able to fix it completely from my phone."

What makes this voice work: specificity, real numbers, real places, real reactions. Not "I was excited" — "my heart was racing." Not "I worked remotely" — "I was at the beach in Altafulla in Spain, by the way, an awesome beach."

### Voice 2: Stoic / Philosophical framing
Short. Aphoristic. Timeless. Used at section openings and transitions.
Model: Seneca's Letters to Lucilius — direct address, concrete image, then the turn to the larger principle.
This voice zooms out. Ed's voice zooms in. Together they create the rhythm.

Examples of RIGHT tone for this voice:
- "Seneca wrote to Lucilius: 'Dum differtur vita transcurrit.' While we delay, life passes. He was not describing the first century. He was describing this one."
- Not philosophical flourish for its own sake — always tethered to a concrete observation.

## Rules

1. **Only write what is in the source files.** If an event, claim, or observation is not in `sources/dictations-01.md` or the AI experiments log, write `[QUELLE FEHLT]` and stop. Never invent.

2. **English throughout.** Ed dictated in English. The essay is in English.

3. **No AI-speak.** Never: "leverage", "unlock potential", "game-changer" (unless Ed said it), "seamlessly", "revolutionary", "paradigm shift" (unless earned). 

4. **Specificity over abstraction.** If you can name the place, name it. If you can name the number, name it. El Chaltén, not "a mountain town." −15°C, not "very cold."

5. **Short paragraphs.** Max 4 sentences per paragraph in Ed's voice. The white space is part of the rhythm.

6. **The Seneca voice is short.** One to three sentences max. Then back to Ed.

## What is NOT allowed

- Inventing anecdotes Ed didn't describe
- Describing emotions Ed didn't express
- Drawing conclusions Ed didn't draw
- Making the language "better" by making it less like Ed
- Philosophical depth that Ed didn't indicate (only Stoic/Seneca as framing, not as argument)
